    Utah's grid has a rush hour, and it comes after sunset

    On a July weekday, demand peaks at 5 PM. Then the sun sets and solar drops away, so the load the rest of the fleet must carry, the net load, keeps climbing to 8 PM.

    3 of 6 · Move vs wait

    Waiting helps. Moving helps far more.

    An 8-hour, 64-GPU job arrives at 7 PM. Run now in Utah: 658 g/kWh. Wait for 9 AM: 439, a third cleaner. Send it to Northwest hydro: 46, which is 93% cleaner.

    5 of 6 · Clean is not rush

    So the router chases clean hours, not quiet ones

    In Utah the dirtiest hours are coal nights, and the rush is the early evening. They barely overlap. An emissions-first router moves work from nights into the midday solar stretch, not out of the 7–11 PM rush.
