Problem and idea

Problem

AI clusters draw power around the clock. Utah's grid (PacifiCorp East) averages about 560 g CO₂/kWh and runs on coal at night. Operators can choose when and where a job runs. Which lever matters, and how much?

Idea

A day-ahead "traffic report" for the grid (Option 1) feeds a router that keeps AI jobs out of dirty hours and regions (Option 2). We replay a real GPU-cluster workload against real hourly EIA carbon data, then add storage for the work that cannot move.

What makes it different

  • Real data: EIA hourly CO₂ for 4 regions, plus a year of a measured GPU-cluster trace.
  • An exact gang-job simulator with site capacity and transfer costs.
  • Honest forecasts that respect publication delays; no peeking.
  • A sealed test year, opened once and logged.
  • Every number labelled observed, modeled, simulated or assumed.
  • An offline, premium dashboard.

Findings so far (validation year, Nov 2024 – Oct 2025)

22.5%CO₂ cut by moving work out of Utah (S: 18.4–26.8%)
0.7%cut by waiting for cleaner hours in Utah alone
3–4%cut if only jobs of ≤ 8 GPUs may move
35%cut if remote spare capacity doubles
  • Moving beats waiting about 30×. The cut holds across every robustness check: 20.2–23.2% on the consumption basis.
  • The big jobs carry it. The top 1% of jobs use 94% of the energy. About 66% of the moved energy goes to Northwest hydro.
  • Forecast choice barely matters. The learned model ties the weekly profile, and a simple fixed clean-region ranking (S) does as well as any dynamic search.
  • Every policy is on time, by construction under baseline bookings, and above the lower bound (3,501 t).

Pitch (draft outline; final after the test results)

Deck, about 8 slides

  1. Utah's AI jobs run on coal nights.
  2. The grid's rush hour isn't when it's cleanest.
  3. Tomorrow's grid traffic report.
  4. One job: stay, wait, or move.
  5. Moving beats waiting by about 30×.
  6. The catch: big jobs must move. Storage covers what can't.
  7. How we know: sealed test year, every number labelled.
  8. What a site operator would do tomorrow.

Demo flow, 4–5 min

  • The Story mode is the spine, one beat per slide idea.
  • The Control room and Cockpit are for Q&A.
